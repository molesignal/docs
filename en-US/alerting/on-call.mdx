An escalation policy decides who is paged for an incident, and in which order. Its steps can name
people, teams, or on-call schedules, so the page follows the rotation instead of a fixed person. Open
**Alerts → On-call schedules** and **Alerts → Escalations**. Reading them requires `schedules.read` and
`alerts.escalations.read`; creating, changing, and deleting schedules requires `schedules.create`, `schedules.update`, and `schedules.delete`, and changing policies requires
`alerts.escalations.manage`.

## How a page reaches someone

1. A rule fires and opens an incident. The incident uses the escalation policy of the rule, chosen by
   [alert routing](/en-US/alerting/routing) when the rule uses the default policy.
2. Step 1 of the policy runs: each target receives a notification event. A user target is that person,
   a schedule target is whoever is on call now, and a team target goes to the team as a whole.
3. [Notify policies](/en-US/notification-channels#create-templates-and-policies) turn each event into
   deliveries: the connectors, the personal order of each person, and the fallback routes.
4. When nobody acknowledges within the **Ack timeout** of the step, the next step runs, until the
   incident is acknowledged or resolved, or the steps run out. With
   [Acknowledged but unresolved](#acknowledged-but-unresolved) set, an incident acknowledged but left
   unresolved goes back to unacknowledged and is paged again.

A silence that matches the incident pauses the escalation while it lasts, and the escalation goes on
after it; see [Notifications during a silence](/en-US/alerting/silences#notifications-during-a-silence).
With several `alert` nodes, each rule belongs to one node, and that node also escalates the incidents
of the rule. See
[Role-based deployment](/en-US/deployment/role-based#work-division-between-alert-nodes).

## On-call schedules

**On-call schedules** lists the schedules with their timezone, rotations, people, who is on call now,
the next handoff, and the state. The head sums up the schedules, the people on call now, the handoffs
this week, and the temporary coverage. Filter by schedule, team, owner, timezone, or state, or show
**Only schedules I participate in**. **Subscribe to my shifts** above the list puts the current user's
shifts across schedules into a calendar app, see [Calendar subscriptions](#calendar-subscriptions);
**Shift swaps** lists the swaps the current user asked for and those asking the current user, with how
many wait for an answer, see [Shift swaps](#shift-swaps).

| State | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Active** | Someone is on call. |
| **Handoff soon** | The next handoff is within 2 hours. |
| **Not started** | The first rotation has not begun. |
| **Paused** | The schedule is paused and puts nobody on call. |
| **Coverage gap** | Nobody is on call now. |

### Create a schedule

1. Select **New schedule**.
2. Under **Basic information**, enter the **Schedule name**, a **Description**, the **Timezone**, such as
   `Asia/Shanghai`, and the **Team**.
3. Under **Rotation rules**, set up each rotation:

   | Field | Use |
   |---|---|
   | Rotation name | For example `primary`. |
   | **Rotation cadence** | **Daily**, **Weekly**, **Workdays** (daily, Monday to Friday), or **Custom period** with a **Period (hours)**. |
   | **Start date and time**, **Handoff time** | When the rotation begins, and the time of day the shift changes. |
   | **Members (rotate in order)** | The people, in the order they take turns. |
   | **Restrict to specific days / hours** | Covers only some hours of the week: each range has its **Days**, a **Start (HH:MM)**, and an **End (HH:MM)**, in the timezone of the schedule, to the minute. **Add range** adds more, such as 9:30–12:00 and 13:30–18:00. An end not after the start runs past midnight and counts for the day it starts; a whole day is 00:00–24:00. |
   | **Holidays** | **Don't count holidays** (default), **Leave holidays out**, or **Holidays only**, by a **Holiday calendar**; see [Holiday calendars](#holiday-calendars). A rotation for holidays put below a weekday rotation that leaves them out covers the days off. |

4. Optionally turn on **Swaps need approval**: after the person asked accepts a swap, someone who
   manages the schedule approves it before it takes effect.
5. Check **Next 7 days**, which previews the order, the handoff times, and the people covered.
6. Select **Create schedule**, or **Save draft** to keep it paused.

When several rotations cover the same moment, the first one in the list that has someone on call
decides. Add a second rotation, for example for nights or weekends, below the first.

Handoffs follow the local time of the schedule's timezone: a rotation that hands off at 09:00 in New York
hands off at 09:00 all year, and the shift across a daylight saving change lasts 23 or 25 hours. A
handoff that falls in the hour skipped when clocks go forward takes the first moment after it; one in
the hour repeated when clocks go back takes the earlier. The preview, the timeline, calendar
subscriptions, and on-call load are all worked out by the server the same way.

### Temporary coverage

A temporary override hands on-call to one person for a fixed window, for leave, a swap, or an
incident. On the schedule, select **Add override**, choose the **Covering person**, enter the
**Reason**, the **Start time**, and the **End time**, and save. An override wins over every rotation
while it lasts.

Two overrides of one schedule cannot overlap. The form warns when the person is on call for another
schedule at the start, and when the start is already past, which makes the override active at once.
**End coverage** ends a running override early.

### Shift swaps

A shift swap asks another person on the schedule to cover a stretch of a shift, without asking a manager
for an override. Under **Shift swaps** in the schedule's details, select **Ask for a swap**, pick one of the
requester's shifts in the next 30 days (or enter a start and end), choose who it is **Taken by**,
optionally add a note, and **Send request**. The requester must be the only person on call on the schedule
for the whole stretch, and a stretch that overlaps an undecided swap cannot be asked for.

| State | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Waiting for an answer** | The person asked can **Accept** or **Decline**, and the requester can **Withdraw**. |
| **Waiting for approval** | The schedule has **Swaps need approval** on and the person accepted; someone who manages the schedule can **Approve** or **Reject**. |
| **In effect** | The schedule has an override with the person who took the shift. |
| **Declined**, **Rejected**, **Withdrawn** | The swap ended and the schedule is unchanged. |
| **Expired** | The stretch ended before the swap was settled. |

Each step notifies whoever needs to know: a request notifies the person asked; a decline, the requester;
a swap waiting for approval, the schedule's creator, its last editor, and the requester; one that takes
effect or is rejected, the requester and the person asked; a withdrawal, the person asked. The built-in
policy [Shift swap: notify the people involved](/en-US/notification-channels#built-in-policies) sends them.
Swaps that took effect show in the schedule's **Change history** with its other changes.

### Calendar subscriptions

**Subscribe to calendar** in a schedule's **More** menu makes a subscription address that puts every shift
of the schedule into a calendar app such as Google Calendar, Outlook, or Apple Calendar;
**Subscribe to my shifts** above the list subscribes to the current user's shifts across schedules. Calendar apps
refresh it about every hour, and it covers 30 days back to 90 days ahead, overrides and swaps included.

The address is shown only once, and anyone who has it can see the shifts. Each read checks the subscriber
again: once the account is disabled, leaves the organization, or can no longer see the schedule, the
address serves nothing. **Reset address** issues a new address and stops the old one at once;
**Stop subscription** stops it. Unlike the `.ics` file from **Export calendar**, a subscription follows
the schedule's changes.

### Schedule details and actions

Open a schedule to see who is on call now and since when, the next handoff, the rotation rules, the
temporary coverage, the [shift swaps](#shift-swaps), a **Next 7 days** timeline, **Member load (last 30 days)**,
and the **Change history** of the schedule. Member load gives how long each member was on call, how many
alert notifications reached them, how many came at night (22:00 to 08:00 in the schedule's timezone) or on a
weekend, and how many nights were broken, the same as **On-call load** in the
[insights](/en-US/alerting/incidents#insights). The menu of
a schedule has:

| Action | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Edit schedule** | Changes the schedule and its rotations. |
| **Add temporary coverage** | Adds an override. |
| **Pause schedule** / **Resume schedule** | A paused schedule puts nobody on call, so steps that target it reach nobody. |
| **Copy schedule** | Creates a copy named `<name> copy`. |
| **Subscribe to calendar** | Makes a calendar subscription address; see [Calendar subscriptions](#calendar-subscriptions). |
| **Export calendar** | Downloads the shifts as an `.ics` file for a calendar. |
| **Delete schedule** | Deletes it. |

[Home](/en-US/home#on-call-activity-and-dashboards) shows the current shift, and members choose how on-call
events reach them in [Account settings](/en-US/account#notify).

### Members who cannot get alerts

Someone on call who cannot get alerts wakes nobody when an incident opens. MoleSignal sorts each member
by the high-urgency steps of their personal settings:

| State | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Reachable | Personal alert notifications are on, and at least one step has an enabled, verified method on an enabled connector. |
| Group only | No personal method, but a team or organization default route reaches a group chat. |
| Unreachable | Neither. |

Unreachable members are marked beside the schedule's members and on each step of an escalation policy;
when the next person on call cannot get alerts, the schedule's page says so at the top. Someone in a
schedule who cannot get alerts sees a notice in [account settings](/en-US/account#notify)
and on the on-call card of the [home page](/en-US/home). Thirty minutes before a shift, when the person
taking it cannot get alerts, the built-in policy [Unreachable on-call member: notify them and the schedule
managers](/en-US/notification-channels#built-in-policies) tells them and the schedule's managers.

When an incident escalates to a step where nobody can get alerts, nobody on call included, it goes to the
next step at once instead of waiting for the ack timeout, and the timeline says
**Nobody at level N could receive the alert, so it went to the next level**. When nobody on the whole
policy can get alerts, the incident stays open and the timeline and the policy page say so.

## Escalation policies

**Escalations** lists the policies with who is told, and when.

1. Select **New policy** and enter the **Policy name**.
2. For each step, choose **Applies from severity** (**Any severity**, or a minimum severity), the
   **Ack timeout** (1, 5, 10, 15, or 30 minutes, or 1 hour), and the **Targets**: a **User**, a
   **Schedule**, or a **Team**. **Add step** adds the next step.
3. Optionally turn on **Repeat the whole policy until acknowledged**, with **Max loops**.
4. Optionally set **Acknowledged but unresolved**; see [below](#acknowledged-but-unresolved).
5. Under **Urgency**, choose how the policy sets urgency; see [Urgency](#urgency).
6. Select **Create policy**.

A step whose minimum severity is higher than the severity of the incident is skipped. The connectors and
the fallback routes of each delivery are set in **Settings → Notify → Policies**, not in the escalation
policy.

### Acknowledged but unresolved

When someone acknowledges an incident and it then stays unresolved, **Acknowledged but unresolved** hands
it back to the on-call: choose 30 minutes, or 1, 2, or 4 hours (the API takes 5 minutes to 24 hours).
When the incident is still unresolved that long after it was acknowledged, it goes back to **Waiting**,
its assignees are notified again, and `{{escalation.reason}}` in a template is `ack_timeout`; when
nobody acknowledges it then, escalation goes on as usual. The default, **Do not notify again**, keeps an
acknowledged incident acknowledged until someone resolves it. A snoozed incident goes back when the
snooze ends instead.

### Urgency

Severity says how bad a signal is; urgency decides whether to wake someone now. Each incident opens with
one urgency level. The two built-in levels behave like this:

| | High urgency | Low urgency |
|---|---|---|
| Escalation | Pages along the steps | Notifies the first step only, never further, never repeats the policy |
| Personal notifications | The person's high-urgency steps | The person's low-urgency steps; without them, the first high-urgency step only |
| Quiet hours | As the person's **Still get high-urgency alerts during quiet hours** says | Never during quiet hours |

An organization can rename the levels, change what each one does, and add more; see
[Urgency and priority levels](#urgency-and-priority-levels).

An urgency set by the rule, the external alert, an alert pipeline, or the person who opens an incident by
hand wins. Otherwise the **Urgency** of the incident's escalation policy decides:

| Choice | Effect |
|---|---|
| **By severity** (default) | Each severity goes to the level the organization put it in: critical and error are high, warning and info low, unless changed. |
| **Always one level** | Every incident gets the level chosen under **Urgency**. |
| **By support hours** | One level **Inside the hours** and another **Outside the hours**, high and low by default. Support hours are weekly ranges in one timezone, and can leave holidays out by a [holiday calendar](#holiday-calendars). With **When support hours begin, raise incidents nobody acknowledged that are less urgent than the inside level, and page again from the first step**, an incident opened at night that nobody acknowledged by morning is raised to the inside level when support hours begin and level 1 is notified again; the timeline says **Support hours began: urgency raised to … and level 1 notified again**. |

**Incidents of a level that stops at the first step climb the steps here too** lets every level escalate
as high urgency does under this policy. An external alert that gives no severity is high urgency, and an
urgency the organization has no level for is dropped when the incident opens, so the policy decides. The
policy list names an urgency choice other than by severity after the chain.

<Note>
Urgency by severity means warning and info incidents notify the first step only and never during quiet
hours. To keep every incident paging along the steps, set the policy's urgency to **Always one level**
with high urgency.
</Note>

### Urgency and priority levels

Open **Settings → Alerting → Priority and urgency** to set the levels of the organization. Reading them
requires `alerts.importance.read`, and changing them requires `alerts.importance.manage`.

**Urgency levels** lists the levels, the most urgent first; the arrows move one up or down. Each level has:

| Setting | Use |
|---|---|
| Name | How the level reads in lists, filters, and messages. |
| **Climbs the steps** | Pages along the steps of the escalation policy; when off, the level notifies the first step only. |
| **During quiet hours** | **Follows the person's switch** (**Still get high-urgency alerts during quiet hours**) or **Never wakes anyone**. |
| **Personal steps** | Which of a person's two groups of steps it uses: **High-urgency steps** or **Low-urgency steps**. |
| **By severity** | The severities that go to it when a policy sets urgency by severity. Each severity goes to exactly one level. |

The built-in levels `high` and `low` can be renamed and changed, not removed. **Add urgency level** adds
up to three more, five in all; the **Key of a new level**, such as `urgent`, starts with a lowercase letter
and holds lowercase letters, digits, and underscores. Rules, external alerts, alert pipelines, and the API
name a level by its key. An incident whose level is removed later behaves as high urgency.

**Priority levels** sets how many priorities the organization uses, 1 to 9, and the name and description of
each. The keys stay `P1`, `P2`, and so on, as integrations and imported files use them; a priority beyond
the levels, from an external alert or an alert pipeline, falls to the lowest one. Templates take the names
as `{{urgency_name}}` and `{{priority_name}}`; see
[Template variables](/en-US/notification-channels#template-variables).

### The default policy

A rule names its escalation policy with `escalation_policy_id`. A rule that names none, as every rule
saved in the rule workbench, uses the alias `default`: [alert routing](/en-US/alerting/routing) picks a
team's on-call policy by the routing rules, the service's owning team and the team label, and otherwise
the policy of the organization named `default`, without regard to case. Without such a policy, those
incidents escalate to nobody.

Create a policy named `default` before the first rule, for example with the primary on-call schedule in
step 1 and the team lead in step 2. Once an incident has started escalating, it keeps the policy it
found, even if the `default` policy is renamed later.

## Holiday calendars

A holiday calendar lists days off and make-up workdays, so weekly hours can leave public holidays out or
apply only on them. Open **Settings → Alerting → Holiday calendars**. Reading calendars requires
`alerts.calendars.read`, and changing them requires `alerts.calendars.manage`. MoleSignal ships no public holidays: they change
every year, so each organization enters or imports its own.

1. Select **New calendar** and enter the **Name** and a **Description**.
2. Under **Days**, pick the **Year** and add each **Date** with its **Kind**, **Day off** or
   **Make-up workday**, and the **Name of the day**. **Import ICS** reads an `.ics` file of public
   holidays: every all-day event becomes a day, and a name that contains 补班 or 上班, or ends with 班,
   becomes a make-up workday. Nothing is saved until the calendar is saved, so check the days first.
3. Select **Create calendar**.

Weekly hours choose under **Holidays** how a calendar counts:

| Choice | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Don't count holidays** (default) | Holidays count like any other day. |
| **Leave holidays out** | The hours do not apply on a day off. A make-up workday counts as a Monday. |
| **Holidays only** | The hours apply on days off only. |

A date is read in the timezone of whatever uses the calendar, and a range that runs past midnight
belongs to the day it starts. Rotations of [on-call schedules](#create-a-schedule), the support hours of
[urgency](#urgency), the time conditions of [routing rules](/en-US/alerting/routing#create-a-rule) and of
[notify policies](/en-US/notification-channels#create-templates-and-policies), alert pipelines, and the
**Holidays** window of [silences](/en-US/alerting/silences#silence-policies) can count by a calendar. A
calendar in use cannot be deleted; the dialog names what uses it instead. A change reaches every node
within a minute, and calendars follow the organization to the other clusters of a federation.

## Import and export

**Import / export** above the list of escalation policies, and above the list of on-call schedules,
moves them between organizations and clusters as a file, as alert rules do:

- **Export all escalation policies** or **Export all schedules** downloads a bundle; with some selected,
  only those. A bundle names what it refers to: users by email, and teams, schedules, escalation
  policies, and holiday calendars by name.
- **Import escalation policies…** or **Import schedules…** takes a bundle as JSON or YAML, tries it first,
  and lists what is created, updated, unchanged, or wrong; **Only create; leave those with a name that
  exists as they are** skips the existing ones. Each item is matched by name, every reference must name something in the
  organization, and nothing is written while any item is wrong.

A schedule in a bundle keeps its rotations and settings, not its temporary coverage or shift swaps;
importing a schedule that exists keeps them. Importing policies requires `alerts.escalations.manage`, and importing
schedules requires `schedules.create`. Routing rules have the same; see
[Alert routing](/en-US/alerting/routing#import-and-export).
