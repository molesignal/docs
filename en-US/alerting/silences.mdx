A silence pauses notifications and escalation for matching incidents, while the incidents are still
recorded. A semantic group collapses related incidents so that responders handle them once. Neither
changes the rules. To hold back lesser incidents because a worse one is active, use
[inhibition](/en-US/alerting/inhibition).

## Silence policies

Open **Alerts → Silence policies**. Each policy shows its state, its window, and what it matches.

| State | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Silencing now** | Matching incidents are silenced. |
| **Not begun** | The window starts later. |
| **Between hours** | A recurring window is outside its hours now. |
| **Ended** | The window is over. |
| **Off** | The policy is disabled. |

To create a policy:

1. Select **New silence policy** and enter a **Name**. Keep **Enabled** on.
2. Under **Match**, select **Add matcher** for each condition on the labels of incidents, such as
   `service` `=` `checkout`. All matchers must match. `=~` and `!~` take regular expressions. Without
   matchers, the policy matches **Every incident**.
3. Under **Window**, choose the **Window type**:
   - **Fixed range**: a **Start** and an **End**;
   - **Every week**: a **Timezone** and one or more ranges, each with its **Days**, a
     **Start (HH:MM)**, and an **End (HH:MM)**, to the minute, for example every Saturday from 02:00
     to 06:00; an end not after the start runs past midnight. Under **Holidays**, the ranges can leave the
     days off of a [holiday calendar](/en-US/alerting/on-call#holiday-calendars) out, or apply on them only;
   - **Holidays**: a **Timezone** and a **Holiday calendar**: every day off of the calendar, all day, such
     as quiet public holidays for a test environment;
   - **Every month**: a **Timezone**, a **Day of the month** (a month without that day takes its last
     day) or **Which** (the first to the fifth, or the last) **Weekday**, a **Starts at (HH:MM)**, and
     how long it **Lasts (minutes)**, up to 7 days. For example the first Sunday of every month from
     02:00 for 240 minutes. A month without a fifth such weekday is skipped.
4. Under **Comment**, enter the **Reason**, then select **Create silence**.

The list gives when a recurring silence next starts or ends, worked out by the server in its timezone,
daylight saving included.

Silences match the labels that incidents carry: the labels of the rule, and the values of the groups of
a [split rule](/en-US/alerting/rules#split-a-rule-by-columns). Incidents of [external alerts](/en-US/alerting/sources)
carry `alert.source=external`, `external.kind` (such as `alertmanager`), the alert's own labels and the
extra labels of the source; `external.kind` `=` `grafana`, for example, silences every alert Grafana sends.
Name the affected service and the end time in every planned silence, and let it end by itself rather than
deleting it later.

<Warning>
  A silence hides notifications; it does not prove that the service has recovered. Check the incident
  list when the window ends.
</Warning>

### Notifications during a silence

While a silence is in effect, matching incidents still open and are recorded as usual. Their
notifications are handled by type:

| Notification | During the silence | After the silence |
|---|---|---|
| Alert triggered, alert escalated | Not sent. Escalation stays at its current step. | For an incident neither acknowledged nor resolved, the triggered notification is sent and escalation goes on; when the acknowledgement timeout of the current step ran out during the silence, the next step is notified right away. Nothing is sent for an incident acknowledged or resolved during the silence. |
| Later steps of a personal order, acknowledgement-timeout escalation of a notify policy | Not sent. | Sent while nobody has acknowledged. |
| Alert acknowledged, alert resolved | Sent when the triggered notification went out before the silence began; not sent when the silence held it back. | Sent as usual. |

A silence ends when its window ends, or when the policy is disabled or deleted.

### Silence one incident

**Silence** on an incident pauses notifications and escalation for that incident only, for **1 hour**,
**4 hours**, **24 hours**, or **7 days**, with an optional **Reason**. It does not disable the rule or
silence other incidents of the same service. The silence appears in **Silence policies**, where it can be
changed or removed early.

Creating, changing, and removing silences requires `alerts.mutes.manage`.

## Semantic groups

Open **Alerts → Groups**. A semantic group collapses incidents that share label values, like the
`group_by` of Alertmanager. Acknowledging or resolving a group through the API does the same to every
incident in it; see [Groups of incidents](/en-US/alerting/incidents#groups-of-incidents).

1. Select **New group** and enter a **Name**, such as `critical-by-service`. Keep **Enabled** on.
2. Under **Matchers**, add the conditions an incident must meet, for example `severity` = `critical`.
   All must match; with none, the group applies to every incident.
3. Under **Group by**, enter the **Group-by labels**, comma-separated, such as `service, region`.
   Incidents with the same values of these labels collapse into one group.
4. Select **Create group**.

A disabled group is skipped. Deleting a group keeps the incidents and removes only the grouping.

**Import** creates groups from JSON: an array of groups, or an object with a `groups` array.

```json
[
  {
    "name": "crit-by-service",
    "enabled": true,
    "matchers": [{ "label": "severity", "op": "eq", "value": "critical" }],
    "group_by": ["service"]
  }
]
```

Managing groups requires `alerts.semantic_groups.manage`.
