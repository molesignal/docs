An incident is opened when a [rule](/en-US/alerting/rules) fires, when a [synthetic check fails](/en-US/synthetics#alert-incidents), or when an [alert source](/en-US/alerting/sources) receives an outside alert. It records the rule, the values, the
labels, and every step until it ends. Open **Alerts → Incidents**, or press `⌘⌥A`. Reading incidents
requires `alerts.incidents.read`; acknowledging, resolving, and snoozing
require `alerts.incidents.acknowledge`; the other actions of [Act on an incident](#act-on-an-incident) require
`alerts.incidents.update`, or `alerts.incidents.escalate` to escalate and merge; and silencing requires `alerts.incidents.silence`. Alert notifications received
through a Feishu app or Slack app can also be acknowledged, resolved, or snoozed on the card; see
[Alert cards](/en-US/im#alert-cards). A text message or a call can be answered with a code or a key; see
[Replies to texts and calls](/en-US/notification-channels#replies-to-texts-and-calls).

## Incident states

| State | List label | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `open` | **Waiting** | Firing, and nobody has acknowledged it. Escalation goes on. |
| `acknowledged` | **In progress** | Someone owns it. Escalation stops. |
| `resolved` | **Recovered** | Over: the rule recovered, or someone resolved it. |
| `closed` | **Closed** | Over. |

An open incident resolves by itself when the rule meets its [recovery condition](/en-US/alerting/rules#recovery-and-missing-data).
An acknowledged incident stays acknowledged until someone resolves it; when its escalation policy sets
[Acknowledged but unresolved](/en-US/alerting/on-call#acknowledged-but-unresolved), it goes back to
**Waiting** after that long and is paged again. A snoozed incident is **In progress**. An incident of an outside
source resolves when the source reports recovery, acknowledged or not; see
[How alerts become incidents](/en-US/alerting/sources#how-alerts-become-incidents). Each rule, or each group of a
split rule, has at most one active incident at a time.

## The incident list

The head of **Incidents** counts the affected services, the incidents that wait for acknowledgement,
and the incidents recovered in the last 24 hours. A summary says how many incidents fire now, how long
the oldest has been firing, and how many rules are evaluated. **Recently recovered** lists what ended
lately.

Switch between **Firing** and **Waiting**, the incidents that have no owner yet; when the current user
belongs to a team, **My teams** lists the incidents [routed](/en-US/alerting/routing) to those teams. On the
right, filter by priority, or show the incidents with **None**. Incidents are listed by severity, then by
priority, then by how long they have been firing. Each row shows the severity, the incident and its
service (with **Low urgency** for a low-urgency incident), the priority, the state, the duration, and the
owner with the routed team, with three actions:

| Action | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Acknowledge** | Takes ownership. The incident becomes **In progress**, and escalation stops. |
| **Silence** | Pauses further notifications and escalation of this incident only. See [Silence one incident](/en-US/alerting/silences#silence-one-incident). |
| **Runbook** | Opens the response guide of the rule, when it has one. |

Tick rows, or all of them from the header, and a bar of bulk actions appears above the list:
**Acknowledge**, **Resolve**, **Snooze**, **Reassign**, **Adjust** (severity, priority, or urgency), and,
with two or more ticked,
**Merge**, which asks for the incident to keep. One request handles up to 100 incidents; those that
cannot be handled, for example because they recovered, are counted with the first reason, and the rest
are handled.

### Open an incident by hand

A problem no rule caught, such as a customer's report or something someone saw, can have an incident too,
which pages and escalates like any other. Select **New incident** above the list (`alerts.incidents.create`):

1. Under **What happened**, enter a **Title** (required) and a **Description** (Markdown works), and
   choose the **Severity** (error by default), the **Priority**, and the **Urgency** (by escalation policy
   by default). Optionally enter the **Affected services**, separated by commas, and **Labels**, one
   `key=value` per line.
2. Under **Who takes it**, choose one:

   | Choice | Effect |
   |---|---|
   | **Route it** | Routed by [alert routing](/en-US/alerting/routing) as a rule's incident is. |
   | **A team** | The team's on-call policy, or the default one. |
   | **An escalation policy** | Notified from step 1 of the policy. |
   | **Named people** | Up to 10 people are notified directly, with no escalation policy. |

3. Optionally tick **Declare it a major incident**, then select **Create incident**; the incident's page
   opens.

An incident opened by hand carries the label `alert.source=manual`, the annotation `created_by` with who
opened it, and its description in the annotation `description`. It skips alert pipelines, is never
deduplicated with another, and never recovers by itself: someone resolves it. **Opened by** on its page
names who opened it, and insights count such incidents together as **Opened by hand**. Mole Agent's
`create_incident` tool opens one too, with one approval.

## Incident details

Select an incident to open its details. The head shows the state, the time it fired, the rule, and how
long it has fired, with **Acknowledge**, **Resolve**, and **More actions**, which holds the actions of
[Act on an incident](#act-on-an-incident) and **Silence**.

| Section | Content |
|---|---|
| **Timeline** | When it fired, who was notified, who did what (acknowledged, reassigned, added responders, escalated, snoozed, changed the severity, merged, resolved, and the steps of remediation, tickets, and major incidents), what an [alert pipeline](/en-US/pipelines/alert-events#what-becomes-of-an-event) changed before it opened, and where (the API, chat, an SMS reply, a phone keypress, Mole Agent), with the times in between. [Notes](#notes) are added at its foot, and the incidents merged into this one are listed under it. |
| **Investigate** | **Open triggering query** replays the exact query in its explorer. **Related logs** and **Related metrics** open the affected context in the same time range. An incident of a RUM figure has **Open in RUM**. |
| **AI root cause** | The analysis of Mole Agent. See [AI root cause](#ai-root-cause). |
| **Cross-signal handles** | The services, trace IDs, and hosts found in the evidence, each a link. |
| **Triggering query** | The query and the values it returned. **Show sample values** shows the sample rows. |
| **Metadata** | The labels and annotations of the incident. |
| **Assignees** | Who the escalation reached, and the team the incident was [routed](/en-US/alerting/routing#routing-on-incidents) to and why; a snooze, a merge into another incident, or a severity set by hand is noted here. |
| **Blast radius** | For an incident that names a service, what the service reaches along the service map. See [The blast radius of an incident](/en-US/apm/insights#the-blast-radius-of-an-incident). |
| **Sample sessions** | For an incident of a RUM figure, the sessions that show it, each with a replay. See [RUM alerts](/en-US/rum/alerts#incidents). |
| **Similar incidents** | Incidents like this one from the last 90 days, and how they ended. See [Similar incidents and recent changes](#similar-incidents-and-recent-changes). |
| **Recent changes** | The deployments, configuration changes, and feature flags reported for its services and the services downstream around the trigger, and its rule if edited the day before. |
| **Remediation** | The remediation actions that apply to the incident, and the runs and approvals requested. See [Remediation](/en-US/alerting/remediation). |
| **Tickets** | The incident's Jira or Feishu Project tickets and their status; tickets can be created here. See [Tickets](/en-US/alerting/tickets). |
| **Major incident** | Declare a major incident, assign roles, join the war room and meeting, split the work into tasks, and post updates. See [Major incidents and postmortems](/en-US/alerting/major-incidents). |
| **Postmortem** | The incident's internal postmortem: **Write postmortem** drafts one from the template, and **Open postmortem** carries on. See [Postmortems](/en-US/alerting/major-incidents#postmortems). |

## Act on an incident

Every action of **More actions** goes on the timeline and is also available through the
[API](#incidents-api). Mole Agent can take them too: adding a note needs the confirmation of the person
who asked, the others a single approval; see [Approvals and executions](/en-US/agent#approvals-and-executions).
Mole Agent can also create tickets, assign major incident roles, add tasks, and draft postmortems (with the
confirmation of the person who asked), declare major incidents and post updates (with a single approval),
and propose remediation runs (always with two approvals).
A recovered incident and one merged into another can no longer be acted on this way, but
[notes](#notes) can still be added.

| Action | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Reassign** | To people, up to 10: they become the assignees and are notified, the incident goes back to **Waiting**, and escalation goes on when nobody acknowledges it. To a team: the team's on-call policy, or the default policy when the team has none, starts again from step 1. To an escalation policy: that policy starts again from step 1, and the team stays. A note can go with it onto the timeline. |
| **Add responders** | The people chosen join the assignees and are notified; the state stays as it is. |
| **Escalate now** | Notifies the targets of the step chosen at once, the next step by default; only the steps that apply to the severity of the incident are listed. An acknowledged incident goes back to **Waiting**. |
| **Snooze** | 1, 4, 8, or 24 hours, or **Custom…** (1 minute to 7 days). A waiting incident is acknowledged first; nothing is sent while it is snoozed, and when the snooze ends, the incident goes back to **Waiting** and its assignees are notified again. **Cancel snooze** ends it at once. |
| **Severity** | Set by hand, neither the rule nor the alert source or the synthetic check changes it any more. Raising a waiting incident notifies again from step 1 at the new severity; lowering only changes the severity. |
| **Priority** | One of the organization's [priority levels](/en-US/alerting/on-call#urgency-and-priority-levels), P1 to P5 by default, or **None**. It changes the priority only and notifies nobody again. Priority is used in the list's column and filter, the conditions of [routing rules](/en-US/alerting/routing), automatic major incidents, the priority mapping of tickets, and insights. |
| **Urgency** | **High urgency** or **Low urgency**; see [Urgency](/en-US/alerting/on-call#urgency). Raising a waiting incident to high notifies again from step 1; lowering only changes the urgency and stops further escalation. Once set by hand, support hours no longer change it. |
| **Merge** | Merges other active incidents into this one; see [Merge incidents](#merge-incidents). |
| **Silence** | See [Silence one incident](/en-US/alerting/silences#silence-one-incident). |

The notifications these actions send are escalation notifications, and `{{escalation.reason}}` in a
template says why: `reassigned`, `responder_added`, `manual` (escalated now), `snooze_ended`,
`severity_raised`, `urgency_raised`, or `ack_timeout` (acknowledged but unresolved too long). Templates
also take `{{urgency}}` and `{{priority}}`. See
[Template variables](/en-US/notification-channels#template-variables).

### Merge incidents

When one problem opens several incidents, for example an outage that fires several rules, merge them
into one main incident. The merged incidents no longer notify or escalate on their own, no longer show
in the incident list or the insights, and are listed under the timeline of the main one. Resolving the
main incident resolves them; when a merged incident recovers first, the timeline of the main one notes
it. Up to 50 incidents are merged at a time, and a merge cannot be undone.

In the details, tick the incidents to merge in under **More actions → Merge**; in the incident list,
tick several and select **Merge**, then choose the main incident.

### Notes

Add a note in the box at the foot of the timeline, up to 4,000 characters, with what was found and what
was done, so whoever takes over sees it at once. A note given with a reassignment goes there too. Mole
Agent reads the latest notes with the incident.

## AI root cause

When Mole Agent is set up with a model provider, it analyzes new incidents in the background and
stores an evidence-based result with the incident. **Generate** asks for one at once, and
**Regenerate** replaces it. Rate a result with **Helpful** or **Not helpful**; the ratings feed
[Quality](/en-US/quality).

Generating requires Mole Agent permissions. Once the Mole Agent token budget of the organization for
the day is used up, automatic analysis pauses, and generation on demand follows the budget setting. See
[Usage and budget](/en-US/agent#usage-and-budget).

## Similar incidents and recent changes

**Similar incidents** finds the incidents of the last 90 days in the organization most like this one, at
most 5, and says why: **same alert** (the same fingerprint), **same rule**, **same services**,
**similar labels**, **similar title** (Chinese titles included), or **same team**. Each shows how long it
lasted, who resolved it, how many notes it has, and the first sentence of its AI root cause, so whoever
takes the incident can see how it was handled last time.

**Recent changes** lists the [changes reported](/en-US/apm/deployments#report-deployments-from-ci) from 30
minutes before the trigger to 10 minutes after, for the incident's services and the services downstream of
them on the service map: deployments (failed and rolled-back ones are marked), configuration changes, and
feature flags, marked when they are downstream, with the pipeline link when there is one. The incident's
rule is listed too when it was edited in the day before the trigger. Without a service map, only the
incident's own services count.

## Groups of incidents

[Semantic groups](/en-US/alerting/silences#semantic-groups) collapse related incidents, for example all
critical incidents of one service, into one group. Acknowledging or resolving a group with
`POST /api/v1/alerts/incident_groups/{id}/ack` or `/resolve` does the same to every member.

## History

**Alerts → History** lists past incidents with their current state, the time they fired and
recovered, and how long they lasted. Search by incident, service, or incident ID, and filter by
severity and state.

## Insights

**Alerts → Insights** shows how the workspace responds, over the last 24 hours, 7 days, or 30 days:

| Part | Shows |
|---|---|
| Summary | How many incidents still need attention, the **Average recovery time** of the recovered ones, and **Rapid recoveries**: the share recovered within 60 seconds, a sign of noisy rules. |
| **Incident timeline** | Incidents by hour for 24 hours, and by day for longer ranges, in the browser timezone, with the peak. |
| **Most affected services** | The services that incidents name most often. Rules need a `service` label for this. |
| **Top firing rules** | The rules that opened the most incidents. Start tuning there. |
| **Response** | The mean time to take an incident (with P50 and P90), the median time to recover, the escalation rate, and the share someone took, against a period of the same length before; the acknowledgement rate of each escalation step; where incidents were first taken (web, API, chat card, SMS reply, phone keypress, or Mole Agent); and taking, recovery, and escalation split by team, service, person, and priority (incidents without a priority left out). Taking an incident is the first acknowledgement; one resolved by a person without being acknowledged counts at the moment it was resolved. |
| **On-call load** | How long each person was on call, how many alert notifications each received, how many came at night (22:00 to 08:00 in the timezone of the person's schedule) or on a weekend, how many nights were broken, and how many incidents each took first and how fast. A schedule's details show the same for its members. |
