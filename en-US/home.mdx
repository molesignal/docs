**Home** is the first page after sign-in unless the personal start page is changed. It shows how
the system stands now: a status strip across the top, the services to look at first and the
incidents going on, then the signals arriving, the synthetic checks, and the on-call shift of the
account. Every member can open it, and each part shows only what the permissions of the account
allow.

## The head of the page

The time range chooses the window of every figure on the page: **Last 1 hour** (the default),
**Last 24 hours**, or **Last 7 days**. The choice is kept in the address (`?window=24h`), so a link
opens the same view. The page reads everything again every minute; the refresh button reads it at
once.

While setup steps are left, a button before the time range says how many. It opens the
[quick start](#quick-start).

## System status

The strip opens with a verdict: **All systems normal**, or how many issues there are. An issue is
an active incident, a check that is failing, degraded or flaky, a service past its warning or
critical line, or a signal that is delayed or failing. The verdict is red when any of them is
severe (a critical or error incident, a failing check, a critical service, a failing signal) and
yellow otherwise. Next to it are the window and the time of the last read, and the state of each
signal: logs, metrics, traces, and profiles.

Each tile opens the page it summarises:

| Tile | Shows |
|---|---|
| **Active incidents** | Open and acknowledged incidents, by severity, and how many are unacknowledged. Opens [Alerts](/en-US/alerting/incidents). |
| **Firing rules** | Enabled rules firing, out of all enabled rules, and how many are pending. Opens the rules. |
| **Healthy services** | Services within their lines, out of all services seen in the window, with the critical, warning and idle ones. Opens [APM](/en-US/apm). |
| **Passing checks** | Running checks that pass, out of all running checks, with the failing, degraded and flaky ones. Opens [Synthetics](/en-US/synthetics). |
| **Error rate** | The share of failed requests over every service, the number of requests, and the trend. |
| **P95 latency** | The 95th percentile of request duration over every service, P50 and P99, and the trend. |

A tile appears only for an account that can open its page. The APM tiles show a dash while no
traces arrive.

## Services

**Services** lists up to eight services: critical first, then warning, then healthy ones by
requests. Each row shows the environment, the health, the requests over the window with their
throughput, the error rate, and P95. A figure past the service's line takes its colour. Select a
service to open it in APM; **View all** opens the service list.

## Active incidents

**Active incidents** lists up to six open and acknowledged incidents, the most severe first and,
within a severity, the one open longest. Each row shows the severity, the first affected service,
how long it has been open, and who it is assigned to. **Acknowledge** acknowledges an open incident
from the list; an account without the permission sees its state instead.

## Data intake

**Data intake** shows, for each signal, its state, when it last arrived, what it took in over the
window bucket by bucket, and the rows in the window. The foot of the panel gives how many streams
received data and how much was stored and received. **Streams** opens [Streams](/en-US/streams).

| State | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Normal** | At least one stream of the signal received data in the last 15 minutes. |
| **Delayed** | Streams of the signal hold data, but none arrived in the last 15 minutes. |
| **Failing** | The intake probe of the node reports a fault. |
| **No data** | The signal has no stream with data in the window. |

## Synthetics

**Synthetics** shows the availability of every check over the window with its trend, and up to four
checks that are failing, degraded or flaky, the worst first. When all pass, it says how many.
**View all** opens the checks.

## My on-call

**My on-call** follows the [on-call schedules](/en-US/alerting/on-call) of the workspace:

- **On call** and the time of the handover while the account holds a shift; otherwise **Next
  shift** with its start and end, when the account has a shift in the next 30 days.
- The person on call now for that schedule when it is someone else, and whom they cover for during
  a temporary override.
- A note when nobody is on call for the schedule now. **Assign now** opens the schedule to add a
  temporary override.
- A note when the account holds a shift but cannot get alerts. **Set up notifications** opens the
  [notification settings](/en-US/account#notify) of the account.
- **Schedules** opens the schedule of the shift.

## Quick start

The button at the head of the page says how many setup steps are left. Select it to open the list:

| Step | Done when |
|---|---|
| **Connect a datasource** | The workspace has a stream other than the [platform streams](/en-US/streams#platform-streams). |
| **Load sample data** | The sample streams, the sample dashboard, and the four sample saved views exist. |
| **Create a dashboard** | The workspace has a dashboard. |
| **Create an alert** | The workspace has an alert rule. |
| **Set up an on-call schedule** | The workspace has an [on-call schedule](/en-US/alerting/on-call). |
| **Add a notification channel** | The workspace has an enabled [connector](/en-US/notification-channels) whose latest test did not fail. |
| **Connect an AI model** | The workspace has an enabled [model provider](/en-US/agent) with an API key. |
| **Automate a pipeline** | The workspace has a pipeline. |

Each step follows the permissions of the signed-in user. A step is left out when they cannot read
its state or open its page, and a step they can read but not complete appears only once it is done.

**Load sample data** is for a workspace with nothing to look at yet. It is not offered once the
workspace has a stream of its own, nor to a user who cannot read the stream list. Once the sample
is loaded, the step stays and shows as done, and **Connect a datasource** is done with it. To
load the sample into a workspace that already has data, send the
[request in the quickstart](/en-US/quickstart#load-sample-data).

**Load** writes a small set of related sample data into three streams: `sample_app_logs`,
`sample_app_metrics`, and `sample_app_traces`. The traces follow calls from `frontend` through
`checkout` and `payment` to `db`, the logs carry the same `trace_id`, and the metrics give the
latency of each service, so a jump from a trace to its logs works at once. Loading also creates a
dashboard over the three streams and four pinned [saved views](/en-US/saved-views) over
`sample_app_logs`, all showing the last three hours, and then opens the dashboard. The data is
written once and dated before the load, so three hours after a load the dashboard and the saved views
show nothing.

Loading again adds data for the current time. It keeps the dashboard and the saved views as they are,
edits included, and creates only those that are missing, so a load that stopped short can be
repeated. Loading requires `logs.write`, `metrics.write`, `traces.write`, `dashboards.create`, and
`saved_views.create`, and all five are checked before anything is written.

## Permissions

Opening Home needs no permission. Each part loads with the read permission of its area.

| Permission | Part |
|---|---|
| `alerts.incidents.read` | **Active incidents**, its tile, and its share of the verdict. |
| `alerts.incidents.acknowledge` | **Acknowledge** in **Active incidents**. |
| `alerts.rules.read` | **Firing rules**. |
| `apm.services.read` | **Services**, **Healthy services**, **Error rate**, and **P95 latency**. |
| `synthetics.monitors.read` | **Synthetics** and **Passing checks**. |
| `synthetics.results.read` | The availability and its trend in **Synthetics**. |
| `streams.query` | **Data intake** and the signal states of the strip. |
| `schedules.read` | **My on-call**. Without it the panel does not appear. |
| `notify.policies.read` | The note in **My on-call** that the account cannot get alerts. |
| `notify.connectors.read`, `agent.models.read`, `pipelines.read` | The matching steps of the quick start. |
| `logs.write` | **Load** in the quick start. |
