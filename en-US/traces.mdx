Open **Traces** to move between complete traces, individual spans, the RED drilldown, and the
service graph.

## Browse traces and spans

The **Traces** tab lists one row per request: how it ended (the HTTP status of its root span, or
**OK** and **Error**), when it started, its service and operation, its duration against the longest
trace of the page, its span count, its error count, and its trace ID. Click **Started**,
**Duration**, **Spans**, or **Errors** to sort by that column. Over the table, a histogram counts every trace the query matches, and a chart beside it
draws the p50, p95, and p99 duration of those traces.

The **Spans** tab lists one row per matching span, with its kind and its own duration. A filter on
a field of a span holds for the span itself there.

The query editor has the modes **Code** and **Builder**. **Code** takes a field query, such as
`status_code = 'ERROR'` or `duration_ns > 500000000` (longer than 500 ms), or SQL against the trace
stream. **Builder** shows the field query as rows of filters, one row for each alternative. Queries run in tabs that can stand side by
side, the field list opens a field analysis, and **Share** copies a link to the query, as on
[Logs](/en-US/logs#query-tabs). The address bar holds the applied query as well, so a copy of it
shares the query ([Share a query](/en-US/logs#share-a-query)).

A comparison on a field of a span keeps the traces with a span that matches it, and `!=` keeps the
traces none of whose spans has the value. `trace_id`, `duration_ns`, `span_count`, and `error_count`
are compared on the trace itself. `in` asks for any of several values:
`service.name in ('checkout', 'payments')`.

`AND` and `OR` join comparisons, `AND` first, and brackets group them, as on
[Logs](/en-US/logs#write-a-query). A comparison keeps its meaning inside an `OR`:
`service.name = 'checkout' OR span_count > 50` keeps the traces with a span of `checkout` and the
traces of more than 50 spans. Text without a field, such as `timeout`, is looked for in the trace
ID, span ID, service, operation, and status. The **Spans** tab and the RED drilldown read the same
query for each span: a span matches `a OR b` when it matches `a` or `b`.

The field list stands beside the results. **Common fields** come first: `service.name`, `name`,
`status_code`, the HTTP status, and `duration_ns`. A field opens on the values the loaded traces
hold. **Filter for this value** adds the value to the query, a query of alternatives put in brackets
first so the value narrows all of it, and a second value of the same field
joins the first in an `in` list, so several values can be ticked. **Filter out this value** leaves a
value out.

**Columns** over the **Traces** table shows, hides, and reorders its columns, and adds fields of
each trace's root span as columns; the eye of such a field in the field list does the same. The
arrangement is kept per stream for each user, and **Reset to default** brings back the columns the
table opens with.

## Inspect a trace

Click a row to open the trace in a drawer over the list. Drag the left edge of the drawer to change
its width, which is kept for the next trace. **J** and **K** move to the next and previous trace of
the list, and the drawer opens the trace's own page as well.

The head of the trace stays in place: the root service and operation, the HTTP status, the error
count, the duration, the start time, the numbers of spans and services, the depth, and the trace ID.
**Related logs** and **Service metrics** open those signals around the trace. **Profiles** opens
the profiles that kept the trace's ID, or the profiles of the minutes around the trace when none
did. **User session** opens the [RUM](/en-US/rum) session the trace served: the session a span
names in `session.id` or `session_id`, or else the session whose browser request carried the
trace's ID in `traceparent`.

**Download trace** at the end of the head saves the trace as a file:

| Format | Content |
|---|---|
| **JSON** | The spans as the [trace API](/en-US/api/web/trace#read-one-trace) returns them. |
| **OTLP JSON** | An OTLP/JSON export request, `resourceSpans` → `scopeSpans` → `spans`, that a collector or the OTLP HTTP intake takes in again. Times are nanoseconds as decimal strings. |

Four views show the spans:

| View | Shows |
|---|---|
| **Waterfall** | The span tree on a time axis, one color per service. A minimap above it zooms the axis to a dragged stretch. Search steps through matching spans, a toggle keeps only failed spans, and a span can be focused with what it called. |
| **Flame graph** | Each span as a block under the span that called it. A double click zooms the axis to a span. |
| **Span list** | Spans grouped by service and operation, with counts, errors, durations, self time, and share of the trace's own time. |
| **Service map** | The services of this trace and the calls between them, with call counts, durations, and failures. |

**Critical path** in the toolbar of the waterfall and the flame graph draws the spans that the
trace's end-to-end time waited on. The path starts at the end of the root span and goes back in
time: at each moment it follows the child span that ended last by then, cut to the span that called
it, and goes on from the moment that child began. A child still running at that moment ran alongside
the one the path followed and is not on the path. Time that no child covers stays with the calling
span. On the waterfall, a line along each bar
marks the stretches of that span on the path, and the other spans fade. The legend gives the length
of the path and the share of it that each service holds. The switch stays on from trace to trace.

The detail of the selected span stands beside the view, and its width can be dragged. It has the
tabs **Attributes**, **Resource**, **Events**, **Links**, **Logs**, **Metrics**, and **Profile**.
**Links** also lists spans of other traces that link to the span. **Logs** shows the log lines that
carry the trace ID, for the span or the whole trace. **Metrics** draws the requests, errors, and p95
duration of the span's service and operation in the minutes around it.

**Profile** shows what the profiler sampled while the span ran. A sample is linked to a span when
the profiler records it with the trace context, as [OTLP profiles](/en-US/profiles) do. With linked
samples, the tab lists the functions that took the most of one profile type by self value,
**Open flame graph** draws those samples, and **Open in Profiles** opens them on the Profiles page.
A flame mark on a waterfall row shows that the span has linked samples. Without linked samples,
**Profiles around this span** opens the profiles of the minutes around the span.

Each trace also has its own page, which a link to a trace opens. If a browser RUM session contains a
session identifier, open **Session traces** to review related backend requests.

## Compare two traces

Click **Pick two traces to compare** over the **Traces** table, then click two rows. The first trace
picked is **A** and the second is **B**. Click **Compare** to open the comparison on the page of A.
In the head of a trace, **Compare with another trace** takes the trace ID of the other one.

The comparison shows both traces, their durations, and how much longer or shorter B took than A.
Three tabs follow, each with the biggest change first:

| Tab | Shows |
|---|---|
| **By service** | The spans and the self time of each service in A and in B, and the change from A to B. |
| **By operation** | The same for each operation of each service. |
| **In one trace only** | The calls that one trace made and the other did not. A span is matched by the services and operations of the spans above it, so the same query made from two places counts as two calls. A missing call counts the spans that it called under it. |

Self time is the time that spans spent on their own work, not the time spent waiting on the spans
that they called. **Swap A and B** and **Stop comparing** stand at the head of the page, and the
address of the page keeps both traces, so a comparison can be shared.

## Service graph and catalog

The **Service Graph** tab derives nodes and edges from trace spans. The graph displays request rate, error
rate, and latency relationships without a separately maintained topology.

The **Service Catalog** groups observed services and operations for navigation from a service to
current traces.

## Intake and propagation

Send traces with OTLP HTTP or OTLP gRPC. MoleSignal preserves standard W3C Trace Context and
OpenTelemetry semantic attributes. Response headers expose `X-Request-Id` and `X-Trace-Id` for
supported application requests.

A [realtime pipeline](/en-US/pipelines#traces) on the stream changes or drops spans after tail
sampling, before they are written. The trace list, with its span and error counts, shows the spans
as they are written.

## Permissions

An organization workspace requires `traces.read`. Platform administrators use
`sys.telemetry.read` for the protected `_sys` telemetry scope.
