Real User Monitoring (RUM) records web and mobile user experience and connects frontend telemetry
with backend observability.

## How data flows

<Steps>
  <Step title="Create an application">
    An [application](/en-US/rum/applications) is the website or the app that sends data. It comes with
    a client token that can only write events to it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Send events">
    The Browser and Flutter SDKs send JSON [events](/en-US/rum/events) to one endpoint. A client without an SDK
    sends the same events with an HTTP request.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Explore the data">
    MoleSignal checks each event, adds the place, the browser, and the device, writes it to the RUM
    streams, and derives the sessions from the views. Search it in the [explorer](/en-US/rum/explorer).
  </Step>
</Steps>

| Endpoint | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `POST /api/v1/rum/events` | Views, actions, errors, requests, long tasks, and vitals, in the [event format](/en-US/rum/events). |
| `POST /api/v1/rum/replay` | Browser DOM or Flutter screen replay segments. |

Both endpoints take the client token of the application. The full list of RUM endpoints is in the
[RUM API](/en-US/api/rum).

## Data model

Each type of event has its own ordinary Logs stream with a fixed schema. Sessions are derived from the views.

| Stream | Rows |
|---|---|
| `rum_sessions` | One row for each session. |
| `rum_views` | One row for each page view or screen. |
| `rum_actions` | One row for each click, tap, or custom action. |
| `rum_errors` | One row for each error, with the id of its [issue](/en-US/rum/errors) in `error.issue_id`. |
| `rum_resources` | One row for each request. |
| `rum_long_tasks` | One row for each long task. |
| `rum_vitals` | One row for each vital. |

Views and sessions are reported again as they change. Each row of the explorer, and each query of the explorer,
sees the latest version of a view or a session. In SQL, `rum_views_latest` and `rum_sessions_latest` are the
streams with only that version (see [Query](/en-US/query#current-versions-of-rum-rows)).

## Overview

**Overview** is where **RUM** opens. It is of one application at a time, in one environment or in all of them, over the
time range in view. One sentence says how many sessions there were, from how many people, and how many of the sessions had
no error; for an app that sends no web vitals it counts the sessions and the people that had no crash instead. Under it are
the Core Web Vitals of a browser application, each on the scale it is graded by (good, needs improvement, poor), with the
mark at the 75th percentile of its views. A vital opens [Performance](/en-US/rum/performance) on it.

Then come the most viewed pages (the screens, for an app) with their views, the 75th percentile of each vital, and the
share of their views that met an error; a map of where the sessions came from, with the countries that sent the most; the
browsers, operating systems, and kinds of device the sessions used; and the elements users were most frustrated by, with
what most of the actions on them were. A row of a table, or a bar, opens the explorer on the rows it counts.

**Open in dashboard** in the header of the overview opens the [built-in RUM dashboard](/en-US/dashboards#built-in-rum-dashboards)
of the application's platform, with the application and the environment of the page already chosen.

The numbers are counted over every matching row by [`/api/v1/rum/aggregate`](/en-US/api/rum#measure-rows). A card whose
counting takes too long says so, and the others stay. The country of a session comes from the IP geolocation database, so
an application that keeps no place with its events has no map.

## Errors

**Errors** lists the errors of an application as issues: the errors that are alike are one issue, with a state (open, investigating,
regressed, resolved, or ignored), a team, a person, and a record of when and in which versions it was seen. An issue that was resolved
becomes regressed when it comes back in a newer version. Each issue opens in a drawer with its trend, where it happens, its restored stack,
its latest sessions, and the people it affected. See [RUM errors and issues](/en-US/rum/errors).

## Performance

**Performance** tells how the pages of a browser application do for the people who open them. One sentence says where LCP, INP, and
CLS stand at the 75th percentile, each in the colour of its grade, with first contentful paint and the time to first byte beside
them. A chart follows the level in front over time, a bar for each level divides the views among good, needs improvement, and poor,
and a table lists the pages (or the browsers, countries, or versions) with the level of each. A row opens a drawer that says what
the level is made of, which elements set it, and which views were worst. Two more views, **Resources** and **Long tasks**, tell
what the pages load and what blocks them, and a view of a browser opens on how its page loaded. See [RUM performance](/en-US/rum/performance).

## Frustration

**Frustration** tells what frustrates the people who use an application. One sentence says what share of the sessions showed a
sign of frustration (rage clicks, dead clicks, and error clicks, or rage taps and error taps in an app), and how many actions
showed each sign in how many sessions. A table lists the elements, pages, versions, or browsers by the sessions they touched. A row
opens a panel with the sessions touched over time, the signs, the versions and pages, and the newest sessions, each of which opens
its replay from five seconds before the sign. See [RUM frustration](/en-US/rum/frustration).

## Analytics

**Analytics** asks what people do across a session, in three views. **Funnel** counts the sessions that did a series of pages
and actions in order, with the share of them that got through each step, how long they took, and the same by browser, system,
device type, version, or country. **Paths** follows the pages sessions opened after a page, or before it, as columns of the
most visited pages. **Retention** counts the people first seen in a day or a week and the share of them seen again in each period
after. The page opens on the last 7 days. See [RUM analytics](/en-US/rum/analytics).

## Alerts

An [alert rule](/en-US/alerting) can watch a figure of an application: errors, crash-free sessions, the share of sessions with
frustration, failed requests, a Core Web Vital at the 75th percentile, and new or regressed issues. The rule reads the figure the
way these pages do, so the number it watches is the number the page shows. Narrow it to an environment, a page, and a version. See
[RUM alerts](/en-US/rum/alerts).

**Alerts** at the end of the row that scopes the overview, errors, performance, and frustration pages counts the incidents going on for the
application, and opens its incidents, its rules, and templates that make a rule in one click. See
[Alert from a page](/en-US/rum/alerts#alert-from-a-page).

## Investigate with Mole Agent

**Investigate with Mole Agent** opens Mole Agent with a new chat about what is in view. It stands in the header of the
overview, errors, performance, and frustration pages, and where one thing is open:

| Where | The question names |
|---|---|
| Header of **Overview** | The application, its environment, and the time range. |
| Header of **Errors** | The errors of the application, in the same scope. |
| Header of **Performance** | The Core Web Vital in front on the Web Vitals view; the speed of the pages on **Resources** and **Long tasks**. |
| A row of the performance table | The views whose page, browser, country, or version is that value, with the 75th percentile of the level in front when the row has enough views. The button shows under the pointer, and always on a touch screen. |
| Header of **Frustration** | What frustrates the users of the application. |
| An issue drawer (**Investigate**) | The issue, its id, and its error. |
| The panel of a frustration group (**Investigate this element**, **Investigate**) | The element and its selector, or the page, version, browser, or system of the group. |
| Header of a session (**Investigate this session**) | The session, from its start to its end. |
| An incident of a rule on a RUM figure (**Investigate with Mole Agent**) | The alert, the application, the time of the incident, and the sessions it was told by. The chat stays the incident's, so its answer can be written back as the RCA. |

The application is attached to the chat, and the question waits in the message box to be read, changed, and sent. Nothing is
sent without a click on send. The button shows to those who hold `agent.use`. Starting the chat needs `streams.query`, or
`sys.telemetry.read` in the `_sys` scope, and records an [investigation](/en-US/agent#investigations) of the application.

Attach an application to any question with `@`: type its name and pick it under **RUM application**. A message is refused when
the sender cannot read RUM. For the time range of the question, or the last 24 hours when it names none, the model is told:

- the sessions and the people, the share of sessions without an error and without a crash, and the sessions that showed frustration;
- for a browser application, the Core Web Vitals at the 75th percentile with their ratings, and the most viewed pages;
- up to three unresolved issues touching the most sessions, with the versions they were first seen in and came back in;
- the alerts going on for the application, to those who hold `alerts.read`.

Mole Agent reads RUM with `get_rum_overview`, `list_rum_issues`, `get_rum_issue`, `get_rum_vitals`, `get_rum_frustration`,
`get_rum_view_waterfall`, `aggregate_rum`, `get_rum_funnel`, `get_rum_paths`, `get_rum_retention`, and the tools of sessions,
actions, errors, and related traces. They read what the pages read and keep the field masking of the reader. The built-in skill
`/rum-triage` walks them in order: the overview, the issues, the slow or stuck views, the journeys through a flow, the sessions,
and the traces of their requests. It cites what it found with links to these pages.

## Explorer

Open **RUM** from the main navigation. **Explorer** lists the sessions, views, actions, errors, resources,
and long tasks of real users. Narrow them with a field query, count them over time in the histogram, and open a row
in a drawer. A workspace that has no application yet shows how to create one. See [RUM explorer](/en-US/rum/explorer).

**Applications** lists the applications, shows their client tokens, and sets what is kept with each event. See
[RUM applications](/en-US/rum/applications).

## Sessions and replay

A session opens from the explorer with its replay, user context, issues, event timeline, and related backend
traces. A session that recorded a DOM replay carries the field `session.has_replay`, so the query
`session.has_replay = true` lists only those sessions.

**Copy link to this moment** in the head of a session copies its address with the replay opened at the moment it is at, and
[playlists](/en-US/rum/playlists) keep the sessions worth watching in a list to go through one after the other.

Treat replay as sensitive user data. Apply collection controls in the SDK, avoid recording secrets
or personal fields, and use the shortest practical retention.

## Errors, source maps, and symbols

Errors arrive with their structured stack frames, and are grouped into [issues](/en-US/rum/errors) by their type, their message, and the first
frames of the application's own code. Upload browser source maps or mobile symbols from
**RUM → RUM settings → Source Maps & Symbols**. MoleSignal uses uploaded debug artifacts to restore
minified, obfuscated, or native frames. Reading artifact metadata requires `streams.read`; upload and
deletion require `streams.configure`.

## Related backend traces

When a request carries W3C `traceparent`, the `resource.trace_id` of the request links directly to backend
spans. A session lists the traces its requests carried. When no request carried a trace ID, session detail searches
the session time window for related traces.

A slow frontend request can link directly to the corresponding service trace, logs, and metrics while
preserving investigation context.

The link also works the other way: the head of a trace on **Traces** offers **User session** when the
trace's ID was sent by a request of a session, and opens that session.

## Client IP behind a proxy

RUM events arrive from browsers and devices on the public internet. By default, MoleSignal records the address of the direct connection. When a reverse proxy or CDN sits in front of MoleSignal, every event would carry the address of the proxy and geography would be wrong. Open **Settings → Client IP recognition**, select a single-value header or a forwarded chain, and list the trusted proxy CIDRs. MoleSignal trusts the header only for connections from those networks. The setting applies to the whole deployment without a restart.

## Permissions

- `rum.write` sends RUM events. Application-bound `msrum_` tokens grant only this permission for one application.
- `streams.query` opens the overview, the explorer, the errors, the performance page, the frustration page, the session page, and the list of applications.
- `rum.manage` sets the state, the team, and the person of an issue. Owners, administrators, and editors have it.
- `agent.use` shows **Investigate with Mole Agent** on these pages and lets `@` attach an application.
- `alerts.read` shows **Alerts** on the overview, errors, performance, and frustration pages, and `alerts.manage` creates a rule from
  there. See [RUM alerts](/en-US/rum/alerts#permissions).
- `api_tokens.manage` creates, changes, and deletes applications, and shows their client tokens.
- `streams.read` lists debug-artifact metadata.
- `streams.configure` uploads or deletes source maps and mobile symbols.
