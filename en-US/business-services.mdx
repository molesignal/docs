A business service is what customers rely on, such as checkout, payments, or sign-in. It depends on
technical services, synthetic checks, alert rules, and other business services. MoleSignal works
out its health from them and keeps a record of the last seven days. The same health can drive the
components of status pages, so the internal view and the public page say the same thing.

Open **Business services** under **Reliability**. The list shows the services in trouble first,
each with its health, the dependencies behind it, the status page components it drives, and a strip
of the last seven days.

## Create a business service

Select **New business service** and set:

| Field | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Name** | 1 to 120 characters, unique in the organization. Use a name customers understand. |
| **Description** | Up to 500 characters. |
| **Dependencies** | Up to 100. Each names a kind, a target, and an impact. |

An organization can have up to 1,000 business services.

## Dependencies

| Kind | Target | Status it passes on |
|---|---|---|
| **Technical service** | A service name, as traces and alerts carry it. | The worst open alert incident whose `service`, `service.name`, or `service_name` label names the service, or whose query saw it. The name is matched without regard to case. |
| **Synthetic check** | A synthetic check. | **Major outage** while the check fails, **Degraded performance** while it is degraded. A flaky check, a check that has not run, and a paused or archived check pass nothing on. |
| **Alert rule** | An alert rule. | The worst open alert incident of the rule. |
| **Business service** | Another business service. | Its health. A dependency that would make a cycle is refused. |

An alert incident passes on its severity: **critical** is a major outage, **error** a partial
outage, **warning** degraded performance. **info** changes nothing. Muted incidents are left out.

| Impact | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Passes its status on** | The status of the dependency becomes the status of the business service. |
| **At most degraded** | Any trouble of the dependency counts as degraded performance. Use it for a dependency the service can work around. |

## Health

The health of a business service is the worst status that its dependencies pass on: **Operational**,
**Degraded performance**, **Partial outage**, or **Major outage**.

- MoleSignal evaluates the services of an organization within seconds of a change of an alert
  incident or a synthetic check, and every minute in any case.
- The detail page lists the dependencies that are not operational, worst first. A technical service
  or an alert rule names up to five of its open alert incidents and counts the rest.
- The strip of the last seven days shows each period in its status. Time before the first
  evaluation is left blank.

## On status pages

A status page component can stand for a business service. In the settings of the component, choose
the service under **Business service** and a mode:

| Mode | Behavior |
|---|---|
| **Follow the health** | The status of the component follows the health. **Notify subscribers** decides whether those changes are sent to subscribers. |
| **Through automation rules** | Changes of health are a source of the automation rules of the page. A rule with the source **Business service** publishes incidents with its delay, approval, and wording. A rule that names no components publishes on the components that stand for the service. |

Automation templates can name the service with `{{labels.business_service_name}}` and its statuses
with `{{labels.business_service_status}}` and `{{labels.previous_business_service_status}}`. See
[Status pages](/en-US/status-pages) for components and automation rules.

Deleting a business service unlinks the components that stand for it and removes it from the
dependencies of other business services. The status of those components no longer changes by
itself.

## Permissions

| Permission | Capability |
|---|---|
| <code>business_services.read</code> | Read business services, their dependencies, health, and linked components. |
| <code>business_services.manage</code> | Create, change, and delete business services. |

The Owner and Admin roles hold both. Linking a component to a business service also needs
<code>status_pages.manage</code>.
