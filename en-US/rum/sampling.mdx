The Browser RUM SDK makes a sampling decision during session creation. The decision remains stable
for that session, in every tab of the application.

## Configure sampling rates

```ts
const rum = init({
  applicationId: 'checkout-web',
  clientToken: 'msrum_your_client_token',
  site: 'https://molesignal.example.com',
  sessionSampleRate: 25,
  sessionReplaySampleRate: 20,
});
```

Both values are percentages from `0` through `100`:

- `sessionSampleRate` controls whether the SDK reports a session and associated RUM events. The default is `100`.
- `sessionReplaySampleRate` applies only after the session is sampled. The default is `0`.

The effective replay rate is the product of both decisions. In the example, approximately 25% of
sessions report RUM data and approximately 5% of all sessions record replay (`25% × 20%`).

<Note>
  Calling `startSessionReplayRecording()` can enable replay for the current sampled session, but the method
  cannot promote a session excluded by `sessionSampleRate`.
</Note>

## Keep a share of sessions at the server

An application can keep a share of the sessions it receives, on top of what the SDK samples, whatever client sent them. Open the
application in **RUM → Applications** and find **Sessions kept**. **Share of sessions kept (%)** is from `0` to `100`; the default is
`100`, which keeps every session.

A session is kept or dropped as a whole. The server decides by a hash of the session id, so every event and every replay
segment of a session meets the same fate on any node, and a session that is kept has all that it did. The sessions that are
dropped leave nothing behind: no event, no row of `rum_sessions`, and no replay, but for their errors (see
[Keep every error](#keep-every-error)).

The share is of the sessions the SDK reported. With `sessionSampleRate: 50` in the SDK and **Share of sessions kept** at `20`, about
10% of all sessions are stored. A value of `0` stores nothing, which suits an application that is paused without
changing its SDKs. The answer of the [intake](/en-US/rum/events#the-answer) says how many events were left out as `sampled`, and an
SDK that reads it sees that the data is not lost. A replay segment of a session that is not kept answers `200` with `sampled: true`.

A change applies to the events that arrive after it. Sessions already stored stay, and a session that began before the change
is kept or dropped by the new share from then on.

A [session a test drives](/en-US/rum/test-traffic) is kept whole unless a filter of the application names `session.type`: a check should not lose its session to a share meant for real users.

### Filters

A filter gives a share of its own to the sessions it describes: every session of production, say, and a tenth of the rest. Add
filters under **Sessions kept**. A filter has a name, a share from `0` to `100`, and up to 5 conditions, all of which a session has
to meet. A filter without a condition describes every session.

A condition looks at one fact of a session and compares it with a list of values: the session **is** one of them, or **is not** any
of them (a session with no value for the fact is not any of them). Values are not case-sensitive, and `*` stands for any text,
so `2.*` is every version of 2. A condition lists up to 20 values, and an application has up to 10 filters.

| Fact | Field |
|---|---|
| Environment | `env` |
| Service | `service` |
| Version | `version` |
| Platform of the SDK | `source` |
| Kind of session | `session.type` |
| Country | `geo.country_iso_code` |
| Browser | `browser.name` |
| System | `os.name` |
| Kind of device | `device.type` |

The filters are asked in the order they are listed, and **the first one that describes a session decides its share**, so a more
particular filter goes above a wider one. The sessions no filter describes are kept by the share under the filters, **Share of the
other sessions kept (%)**. Without filters, that is the one share of the application.

<Note>
  A session has to meet the same fate in every event, so a condition can only look at facts that stay the same while a session lasts.
  What a person does during a session, and who they are when the SDK only learns it later (a user id set after sign-in), cannot
  decide whether the session is kept. Errors are kept by a switch of their own, below.
</Note>

A replay segment is judged by what it says of its session: the browser and the place it came from, and the `env`, `service` and
`version` that the browser and Flutter SDKs send in the body of the request (an SDK leaves out the `env` or `version` it was not
given). When the segment leaves a fact open, it is kept if any filter that might describe the session would keep it.

### Keep every error

The errors of the sessions that are not kept are stored all the same, unless **Keep every error** is turned off. That keeps a rare error
from being lost with its session: it is an issue, and counted with the others. Such an error is a row of `rum_errors` alone. Its
session has no row, no views and no replay, so the page of the issue says *Session not kept* where it would link to the session.

### Totals are estimates

Every row says the share of such rows that was kept in `retention.rate` (`25` for a session kept by a filter of 25%), and what kept it
in `retention.filter`: the id of the filter, `default` for the share of the other sessions, or `errors` for an error of a session
that was not kept. While **Keep every error** is on, every error is kept, so an error says `100` whichever session it is of.

A page that shows totals counts each row for as many as its share says: a session kept with a share of 25 counts for four. The page says
**Estimated** and the share of sessions that was kept, so a total is what there would be had every session been kept.

- Counts, sums and averages are scaled, and a percentile is weighed by the same shares.
- The people that were seen, and the smallest and the largest values, are not scaled: they are what was seen.
- A funnel and the paths count sessions by their shares. The cohorts of retention are people, so they are counted as they were seen.
- The explorer lists the rows that were kept, and its counts and charts count those rows as they are. Pass `estimate=false`
  to the [aggregate API](/en-US/api/rum#measure-rows) for the same numbers.
- An alert rule that waits for **minimum samples** waits for rows that are stored, not for what they stand for.

Sampling adds noise: a total made of a few sessions says little, however far it is scaled.

## Choose initial values

| Traffic profile | Session rate | Replay rate within sampled sessions |
|---|---:|---:|
| Development or controlled QA | `100` | `100` |
| Low-volume production | `100` | `10`–`25` |
| High-volume production | `10`–`50` | `1`–`10` |

These are starting points, not universal defaults. Estimate event volume, object storage, retention,
and the number of sessions needed for each browser and release before rollout.

## Session boundaries

The SDK stores the decision in the session cookie `_msrum_s_<applicationId>`, which the tabs of the application share.
A new session starts after either boundary:

- `sessionInactivityTimeout`: 15 minutes by default;
- `maxSessionDuration`: 4 hours by default.

Both values accept milliseconds. The allowed range is one minute through 24 hours.

```ts
sessionInactivityTimeout: 15 * 60_000,
maxSessionDuration: 2 * 60 * 60_000,
```

Changing a sampling option does not change the decision already stored for the current session. Call
`stopSession()` or clear the `_msrum_s_<applicationId>` cookie when testing a new configuration.

## Bound client-side volume

Use transport controls to keep memory and request sizes predictable:

```ts
flushInterval: 5_000,
batchSize: 50,
replayFlushInterval: 10_000,
replayBatchSize: 100,
maxQueueSize: 1_000,
```

Monitor intake rate, replay storage, browser overhead, and investigation coverage after each
change. Roll out sampling changes gradually by application and release.
