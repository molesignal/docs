The **Insights** card of the APM overview and of a service's overview says what is worth a look in the
time range: what moved against the stretch before and against the same stretch a week before, the
versions that did worse, and where time could be won back. A finding names the changes near when it
began, and **Investigate** hands it to Mole Agent.

## Líneas base

Above the findings, two lines compare the services in view with two stretches of the same length:

- **En comparación con el período anterior**: the stretch that ends where the range begins;
- **Against the same stretch a week before**: the range moved back seven days.

Each line says how throughput and the error rate moved, the error rate in percentage points. A stretch
without requests has no line.

Minute aggregates are kept for 24 hours. A stretch that begins earlier is read from the hourly
aggregates that cover it, and its requests are counted for the stretch's own length.

## What insights find

| Análisis | Gravedad | Cuándo |
|---|---|---|
| **Cambio** | **Crítico** while the service is critical in the range, else **Advertencia** | The error rate, P95, or throughput moved against each baseline stretch that has enough requests to judge by, and at least one has them. |
| **Reaparecido** | **Advertencia** | A version first seen in the range did worse than the version before it, by the verdict of [Deployments](/es/apm/deployments). |
| **Cuello de botella** | **Sugerencia**; **Advertencia** while the service is not healthy | One dependency takes at least 40% of the time of the service's requests, over at least 20 calls. |
| **N+1** | **Sugerencia** | The same call, other than to a message broker, is made at least 10 times a request and at least 200 times in all: a loop that one call could replace. |
| **Consultas SQL lentas** | **Sugerencia** | A database statement has a P95 of at least 100 ms over at least 20 calls. The two statements of a service that take the most time in all are named. |
| **Endpoint con alta carga** | **Sugerencia** | One endpoint takes at least 40% of the service's time, over at least 50 requests, while two or more endpoints served. |
| **Registros de errores** | **Crítico** at ten times the busier baseline, else **Advertencia** | The service's error-level log lines a minute rose by the MAD detector of anomaly rules (three robust deviations) against the twelve parts of the stretch before and of the same stretch a week before, to at least twice the busier of the two, by at least 20 lines. Logs name a service only by its name, so the lines go to the service of that name in view with the most requests. Up to four log streams with a service and a level field are read; a range they cannot be read in within three seconds goes without this insight. |

A move is judged on each figure on its own:

| Figure | Moved when |
|---|---|
| Error rate | It rose by at least two percentage points, and to at least twice what it was. |
| P95 | It rose to at least 1.5 times what it was, and by at least 100 ms. |
| Throughput | It fell below half, or rose to at least twice, with at least 100 requests in the busier stretch. |

A stretch needs at least 20 requests for its error rate or P95 to count. The finding gives the
figure now beside the figure of each baseline stretch.

The findings come critical first, then warnings, then suggestions, at most 30. Suggestions say where
time goes; they do not say that the service is unhealthy.

### When a move began

A move began at the first bucket of the range that stands past the line halfway between the baseline
and the figure now, when the next bucket stands past it too and at least half of the buckets after it
do. A single bad bucket does not start a move. The card gives the time a move began next to the
finding; the exact time shows when pointed at.

## Changes near a finding

A moved or regressed finding lists the changes from 30 minutes before it began until 10 minutes
after, the nearest first, at most five:

- the deployments, configuration changes, and feature flags that pipelines reported for the service,
  or for a service it calls;
- the versions that the service's spans first carried then, which no pipeline reported, marked as
  first seen in spans.

A change reported for an environment counts only in that environment. Report configuration changes
and feature flags as deployments with a `kind`; see
[Report configuration changes and feature flags](/es/apm/deployments#reporta-cambios-de-configuración-e-indicadores-de-funcionalidad).

## Where insights show

| Page | What the card holds |
|---|---|
| **Overview** | The findings of every service in view, each with its service. **Regressed** is left out: the recent deployments card names the versions that did worse. |
| A service's **Overview** | The findings of the service. **Bottleneck** and **Hot endpoint** are left out: the time spent downstream and the endpoints that weigh most are on the same page. |

When nothing stands out, the card says so. The environment and version filters of the page apply.

## Investigate with Mole Agent

**Investigate** opens Mole Agent with a new chat about what you are reading:

| Where | The question names |
|---|---|
| The header of a service page | The service, its environment, and the time range. |
| An endpoint drawer | The endpoint, its service and environment, and the time range. |
| An error drawer | The error, its fingerprint, its service and environment. |
| A finding on the **Insights** card | The finding as the card says it, its service and environment, and the time range. |

The service is attached to the chat, and the question waits in the message box to be read, changed,
and sent. The button shows to those who hold `agent.use`. Starting the chat needs `apm.services.read`, or
`sys.telemetry.read` in the `_sys` scope, and records an investigation of the service; see
[Investigations](/es/agent#investigaciones).

An attached service gives the model, for the chat's time range or the last hour when it has none:

- each environment's health with its reasons, requests, error rate, P50, P95, P99, Apdex, and version;
- the figures of the stretch before and of the same stretch a week before;
- up to eight findings of the **Insights** card, with the changes near them;
- up to six services that call it, and up to six things it calls, with their health.

A service without APM data gives the calls between services that the service graph paired over the
last day instead.

Mole Agent can also read insights with `get_apm_insights` and what a service reaches with
`get_apm_blast_radius`. The built-in incident triage skill uses both, and the latency regression skill
reads the insights of the service first.

## El alcance del impacto de un incidente

Cuando un incidente nombra un servicio, su detalle tiene una sección **Alcance del impacto**. El servicio se toma de las
etiquetas `service.name`, `service_name`, `service` o `svc`; si no, de los servicios afectados del incidente.
El entorno se toma de la etiqueta `deployment.environment` o `environment`. La sección lee el
mapa de servicios de APM desde 30 minutos antes de que se abriera el incidente hasta que se resolvió, o hasta ahora mientras está
abierto:

| Lista | Qué contiene |
|---|---|
| **Servicios que lo llaman: a cuáles puede afectar el problema** | Los servicios que llaman al servicio y los que los llaman a ellos, hasta tres llamadas de distancia. |
| **Servicios a los que llama: dónde puede originarse el problema** | Los servicios, bases de datos, cachés, endpoints externos y temas a los que el servicio llama o en los que publica, y lo que llaman esos servicios, hasta dos llamadas de distancia. |

Cada lista pone primero lo que no está sano, como máximo seis, cada uno con la distancia a la que está, su estado y las
llamadas entre él y el nodo por el que se alcanza, con la proporción que falló. Un tema o un endpoint
externo no se sigue más allá, y una llamada a través de un tema no llega a su productor, ya que un
productor no espera a sus consumidores. Las llamadas que ya no existen respecto al tramo anterior se omiten.

**Ver en el mapa de servicios** abre el [mapa de servicios de APM](/es/apm/service-map#el-mapa-de-servicios-de-apm) del
entorno con el servicio seleccionado. La sección se muestra a quienes pueden leer datos de APM, una vez que el mapa se
ha leído. Cuando el servicio no está en el mapa en ese periodo, la sección lo indica.

## Alertar sobre anomalías de métricas de APM

Una regla de anomalía puede vigilar una métrica de APM en lugar del resultado de una consulta. Se activa cuando la métrica
en la ventana de la regla se desvía de la misma ventana en cada uno de los días anteriores.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Abrir el editor de reglas de anomalía">
    En una página de servicio o en el panel lateral de un endpoint, abre **Nueva alerta** y elige **Alerta de anomalía**. El
    editor se abre con la fuente de datos **Métrica de APM** y el servicio, el entorno y el endpoint ya completados.
    Una nueva regla de anomalía en **Alertas** ofrece la misma fuente de datos.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Elegir la métrica y la línea base">
    Elige la métrica, luego la ventana retrospectiva de 1 a 30 días, el algoritmo y la sensibilidad. Con
    **Estacionalidad semanal**, solo cuenta el mismo día de la semana.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Guardar">
    Ponle nombre a la regla y créala. Para crear una regla se requiere `alerts.rules.manage`.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Una regla de anomalía admite todas las métricas de APM excepto **Nuevos grupos de errores** y **Tasa de consumo del SLO**. Una ventana pasada
de más de 24 horas se lee de los agregados por hora que la cubren, con las solicitudes y los errores contados
según la duración de la propia ventana. La regla lleva las mismas etiquetas que una regla de umbral sobre la métrica; consulta
[Alertar sobre métricas de APM](/es/apm#alertar-sobre-métricas-de-apm). Para los algoritmos y sus parámetros, consulta
[Alertas](/es/alerting).
